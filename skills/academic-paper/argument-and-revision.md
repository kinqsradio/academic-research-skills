# Argument and revision guide

## Before drafting

Keep a small working table in a named `docs/planning/<topic>/` subfolder with its own GUIDELINES.md:

| Section or claim | Role in the argument | Paper ID, evidence and locator | Limitation or open question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research question | Defines what the paper will establish | Assignment and project brief | Scope still to settle |
| Main claim | States the proposed contribution | Read source, data or derivation | Evidence boundary |

The table is a planning aid, not a requirement to add tables to the manuscript. Use it to detect unsupported conclusions and sections that do not advance the question. An unknown field remains unknown.

When drafting, separate four kinds of statement: what a source reports, what the team's own completed work shows, what follows by reasoned inference, and what future work proposes. Explain the connection where the inference is not direct. Retain uncertainty when the evidence requires it; adding a hedge does not repair a claim with no support.

## Handling comments

When studies disagree, compare their populations, designs, measures, conditions, and uncertainty before proposing an explanation. Preserve unresolved disagreement. Do not select the preferred result or average incompatible measures merely to make the argument simpler.

Maintain a tracker alongside a substantial response or revision:

| Comment and source | Manuscript location | Action or rationale | Status and evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exact issue or faithful summary | Section/page | Edit, verify, discuss or disagree | Open / changed / verified / unresolved |

Preserve the original comments. A change becomes **verified** only after checking the revised manuscript against the requested outcome. A response letter stating that something was changed is not verification. For disagreement, give the evidence and practical reason without pretending the reviewer accepted it.

Revise the abstract, tables and conclusions when a change to the body affects them. Keep response-letter locations aligned with the current version. Record unresolved comments, evidence gaps and deliberate limitations in the delivery note.

## Clear, specific prose

Use [prose-quality-review](../prose-quality-review/SKILL.md) for a focused review with located findings and explicit meaning-preservation checks. The drafting defaults below remain shared guidance.

Treat a request to remove “AI slop” as an editorial request for precise, useful prose. Diagnose the actual problem: vague actors, unsupported praise, repeated framing, or a transition that does not explain the relationship between claims. Do not infer authorship from vocabulary or punctuation, promise to defeat a detector, or alter required AI-use disclosures.

Revise meaning before surface style:

- Name the actor, action, object, and relevant condition when the evidence identifies them. Check overloaded noun phrases and nominalizations; replace abstract noun chains with a direct statement of what happened or is proposed when that improves precision. Never supply an unknown actor or result to make a sentence sound concrete.
- Remove boilerplate openings, self-praise, and generic importance claims unless they do necessary argumentative work and have support. Replace a generic transition with the actual connection, such as a different population, an incompatible measure, or a limitation on inference.
- Check repeated sentence openings, balanced lists, and contrast formulas across paragraphs. Keep a pattern when it expresses a real relationship; revise repetition that obscures the argument. Do not impose word bans, punctuation quotas, forced synonyms, or random sentence-length variation.
- Prefer familiar wording when it preserves the technical meaning. Retain necessary disciplinary terms and define them for the intended audience. A statistical term such as “significant” cannot be replaced casually with “important”.
- Choose active or passive voice according to the topic and responsibility that need emphasis. Make the actor explicit when responsibility matters; passive voice can be appropriate when the procedure or object is the focus. Keep related subjects, verbs, and objects close enough to follow.
- Preserve uncertainty, attribution, scope, numerical values, and the distinction between proposed and completed work. Concision must not remove a qualification that changes the claim.

These are team editorial defaults, informed by the sources below, rather than an authorship test or an experimentally established cure for model-generated prose. The brief, venue, and explicit author requirements take precedence. Preserve source titles, quotations, code, mathematical notation, and established technical spellings during a prose pass.

## Punctuation by function

Check sentence structure and intended meaning before changing a mark. The table distinguishes grammatical problems from house choices; a consistent permitted convention does not need “correction”.

| Mark | Working rule and scope |
| --- | --- |
| Hyphen (`-`) | Join words acting together as a compound modifier before a noun when needed for clarity, as in `evidence-based assessment`. Do not routinely hyphenate an `-ly` adverb plus participle, as in `carefully designed study`. Established compounds can retain a hyphen after the noun; consult the relevant dictionary or disciplinary convention rather than mechanically removing every post-noun hyphen. |
| En dash (`–`) | Use an unspaced en dash for compact numerical or page ranges (`10–15`) and relationships between coordinate terms (`student–teacher ratio`) where the chosen style permits. In running prose, `from 10 to 15` and `between 10 and 15` are often clearer. Do not mix the constructions as `from 10–15` or `between 10–15`. Preserve minus signs in mathematics. |
| Em dash (`—`) | An occasional unspaced em dash can mark an emphatic aside or interruption. A consistent spaced en dash is also valid when the prescribed style uses it. Use one convention for parenthetical dashes throughout. If stacked asides interrupt the argument, restructure the sentence; frequency alone is no error or evidence of AI authorship. |
| Comma (`,`) | Use syntax and meaning, rather than a breathing pause, to decide placement. Separate list items and introductory elements where the structure requires it; use a comma before a coordinating conjunction joining independent clauses. Set off nonrestrictive material with paired commas when needed; do not set off a restrictive clause that identifies the intended subset. Do not join independent clauses with only a comma. Follow the chosen serial-comma policy consistently and resolve ambiguous lists by repunctuating or rewriting. Serial-comma preference is a house choice. |
| Semicolon (`;`) | Join closely related independent clauses, including before a linking adverb such as `however`, or separate complex list items that contain commas. Outside such lists, each side must stand as a sentence. Use a full stop when the clauses are easier to follow separately. |
| Colon (`:`) | In ordinary running prose, use a complete clause before a colon that introduces an explanation or list. Do not insert it between a verb or preposition and its complement. Headings, labels, table stubs, displayed-list lead-ins, and mathematical notation have different conventions; follow their applicable style. |

The following examples are invented language exercises, not reported research findings:

- Vague proposal: “This innovative framework will revolutionise evaluation.” Specific proposal: “We propose comparing the two scoring procedures on the same responses.” The revision describes proposed work without inventing a benefit.
- Wordy proposal: “We will conduct an investigation into the effects of missing ratings.” Direct proposal: “We will examine how missing ratings affect the estimate.” Retain the actual estimand and design in a real draft.
- Comma splice: “The protocol is a proposal, no data have been collected.” Correction: “The protocol is a proposal; no data have been collected.” A full stop is equally grammatical.
- Colon placement: use “The plan specifies two checks: source identity and claim support”, rather than “The checks are: source identity and claim support” in running prose.
- Restrictive meaning: “Reviewers who read the appendix can check the derivation” identifies a subset. “Reviewers, who read the appendix, can check the derivation” presents appendix reading as supplementary information about the reviewers. Do not swap these forms without checking the intended meaning.

## Editorial sources and limits

Consulted on 2026-10-20. Locators below are page headings or numbered rules. These sources inform the defaults above; they do not establish a universal journal style. The Australian Government Style Manual is government house style, and its preferences can differ from a publisher's. No source PDF was downloaded for this guidance.

| Source and precise locator | Rationale and limit |

| Australian Government Style Manual, [Hyphens](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/hyphens), “Hyphenate some but not all compound words”, “Adjectives with verbs”, and “Don’t hyphenate ‘-ing’ and ‘-ed’ verbs or most ‘-ly’ adverbs” | Supports compound-modifier distinctions and checking established spellings; it does not justify banning compound adjectives. |
| Australian Government Style Manual, [Dashes](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/dashes), “Use an en dash for spans in technical content”, “Join nouns with an en dash to show an equal relationship”, and “Em dash” | Supports range/relationship distinctions and recognises both parenthetical dash conventions. | Australian Government Style Manual, [Commas](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/commas), “Set off non-essential clauses”, “Don’t use commas to ‘splice’ sentences”, and “Restrict the use of the Oxford comma” | Supports meaning-sensitive comma checks and illustrates that serial-comma treatment is a style choice. |
| Australian Government Style Manual, [Semicolons](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/semicolons), “Avoid using a semicolon to link sentences” and “Use a bullet or numbered list instead of semicolons in a complex sentence”; [Colons](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/colons), “Start lists with a colon” | Supports clause/list distinctions and avoiding a colon inside a flowing sentence. | Australian Government Style Manual, [Plain language and word choice](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/writing-and-designing-content/clear-language-and-writing-style/plain-language-and-word-choice), “Use everyday words”, “Choose simple words, not complicated expressions”, and “Keep words and phrases with special meaning to a minimum” | Supports clear wording while allowing audience-appropriate specialist terms. | Nature Scitable, [Effective Writing](https://www.nature.com/scitable/topicpage/effective-writing-13815989/), discussions of precision, conciseness, strong verbs, and active/passive voice | Supports precise wording, related sentence elements, and choosing voice by topic. This archived educational page is no longer updated; it is writing guidance, not evidence about AI authorship. |
| Reinhart et al. (2025), [Do LLMs write like humans? This bounded older-model corpus supports checking genre fit and overloaded constructions, not universal diagnosis or word bans. |

This narrow abstract-level evidence does not test punctuation as an authorship signal or support claims about all detectors. The rule against authorship allegations is a review boundary. |

## Final check

Confirm that citations lead to the intended works, quoted or numerical claims match the read material, methods and results describe the same study, the conclusion stays within the evidence, and the requested format and length are met. Apply the clear-prose and punctuation checks above without changing protected source material or weakening qualifications. Check declarations against actual facts and applicable requirements. Report which checks were performed and which require unavailable sources, tools or human judgment.

## Reference maintenance

Keep an artifact-specific working map of IEEE reference number to stable paper ID and verified entry in the [reference catalog](../../papers/REFERENCES.md). Number entries in first-citation order and reuse a source's number throughout the manuscript. When moving or removing text, recheck the citation order, reference list, abstract, tables, and figure captions. Never renumber catalog IDs to match a draft. Verify local files and access limits through the [download register](../../papers/DOWNLOADS.md); follow [paper guidance](../../papers/GUIDELINES.md) for any new download.
