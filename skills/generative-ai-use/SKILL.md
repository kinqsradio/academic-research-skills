# Generative AI use

Produce a specific, readable account led by the human author of the assistance actually used in the identified submission, foregrounding documented author guidance, decisions, revisions, and checks alongside the AI contribution. Read [folder guidance](GUIDELINES.md). Use this workflow during final-submission preparation and for explicit disclosure requests; it does not itself authorize research, manuscript drafting, or submission.

## Establish requirements before composing

Identify the exact manuscript candidate, artifact type, destination, existing disclosure, appendix sequence, length limit, author group, and authorized scope. Read the applicable assessment brief, venue instructions, and university or institutional AI-use requirements, checking their current official versions and retaining source links, dates checked, and section locators. Resolve differences by their stated scope; flag unresolved conflicts for the human author. Do not assume a general research page overrides a course brief or that thesis rules govern coursework or journal articles.

If requirements are unavailable or their application is uncertain, record that gap and continue safe evidence preparation and a visibly provisional draft. Do not claim compliance or finalize affected declarations. Distinguish missing required evidence or confirmation from optional unknown metadata: an unavailable model version or historical prompt is not a universal blocker when the applicable rules permit an unknown field, summary, or representative excerpts. Preserve those limits without claiming a complete history. Apply a four-page limit only when the actual brief requires it. Preserve the required wording and location when a form, footnote, acknowledgements section, or declaration is prescribed; an appendix alone may be insufficient.

Disclosure does not authorize prohibited assistance, cure a policy breach, provide ethics or privacy approval, or replace the author's decision. Surface apparent prohibited use with its evidence and applicable requirement without fabricating a resolution. If applicable rules prohibit AI drafting of the disclosure itself, provide a factual use inventory or permitted outline for human writing instead; do not assume an appendix is exempt. AI tools cannot be authors. Human authorship is distinct from who generated particular wording: keep the human as author while accurately disclosing AI-generated or substantially AI-drafted material that was used or adapted. Neither human authorship nor later revision establishes that every sentence was human-written or human-verified. Keep human contribution and responsibility statements evidence-based and subject to the required author confirmations.

## Build a bounded use record

Read only authorized supplied or available records relevant to this artifact: author-provided accounts, selected conversation exports, dated notes, manuscript versions and diffs, code changes, and existing use records. State the sources and time span examined. Identify selected example interactions as samples rather than a complete register. Do not search unrelated private chat, email, or account history merely to make a disclosure appear complete. Missing logs do not establish no use.

Use [USE_RECORD_TEMPLATE.md](USE_RECORD_TEMPLATE.md) when a working record is needed. Link each material use to its evidence and the exact affected sections, figures, tables, references, code, or analyses. Record:

- Tool, provider, model or version when actually known, and date or supported date range; leave unknown fields explicit.
- Purpose, input type and safe input reference; distinguish minor grammar/clarity editing from substantive ideation, literature synthesis, drafting, translation, coding, data analysis, or figure generation.
- Prompt evidence: label an authentic exact excerpt, a summary, or an author reconstruction. Never invent a verbatim prompt or reconstruct a log as if it were original.
- Actual output used, adapted, or excluded, its location in the identified candidate, and subsequent edits. Record the author's decisions and revisions separately from AI actions, with evidence or explicit confirmation status. Explain material uses that influenced reasoning even if their original wording was discarded.
- Workflow order and meaningful stages, with a safe evidence locator for each stage and selected interaction. For responses as well as prompts, distinguish exact excerpts from summaries, reconstructions, and unavailable records.
- Checks actually completed, who performed them (human or agent), their evidence and limits, and unresolved checks. Tool availability or a planned check is not execution evidence.
- Author account and confirmation status, including collaborators and all-author confirmation where required. Keep status pending until explicit confirmation is recorded.

Include AI assistance used to prepare the disclosure itself when applicable. One record may cover a continuous disclosure-drafting session and its revisions; do not create an infinite chain of records about recording. Keep separate uses separate when tool identity, purpose, treatment, or affected material differs; combine repeated similar uses only when the summary remains accurate and traceable. Do not invent contribution percentages, unused-output claims, no-use statements, approvals, or author declarations.

Store only the necessary, permitted evidence. Exclude credentials, personal or participant data, confidential text, and unauthorized copyrighted passages from both the appendix and Git. Use labelled redactions and safe references to authorized access-controlled records; state their access limits. A requirement to retain prompts does not authorize uploading protected material or publishing restricted transcripts. If redaction limits verification, retain that limitation and identify the human decision needed.

## Compose the submission section

Use [APPENDIX_TEMPLATE.md](APPENDIX_TEMPLATE.md) as a scaffold, adapting its content and format to the actual requirements. Default to canonical Markdown beside the manuscript in its existing `submissions/drafts/<topic>/` folder; integrate into the manuscript only within authorized scope. Follow destination guidance and add meaningful folder guidance if a new maintained folder is necessary. Keep the detailed use record in the appropriate purpose-based `docs/` folder rather than attaching the entire log to the submission.

Find and update the intended existing disclosure rather than appending a duplicate. Match its appendix letter, heading depth, numbering, and style: use `Appendix B. Generative AI Use` and B.1–B.4 when B is the next letter. The default is the four-part structure below. If a mandatory brief or venue format instead calls for a concise declaration or different structure, follow that format and record the reason in the working record; do not force this appendix into it. Honor actual page/word limits, required wording, and placement.

1. **Tool and principle.** Identify actual tools/providers, supported dates, and the role AI played in the author's process. State model/version when known or required, preserving optional unknown metadata in the record. Describe the working principle only to the extent supported: do not borrow claims that every stage was human-directed or every output checked from a style example. Prefer active first-person author voice appropriate to the artifact and author group, explaining documented guidance, choices, and revisions before describing how AI supported them. Do not invent human actions or integrity declarations, imply sole human wording, or obscure substantive AI drafting.
2. **Workflow.** Use a table with `Stage | What I did | What AI did | Check applied` (or `What we did` for an author group, or `What the author did` when first person is inappropriate). Derive the meaningful stages and their order from the actual record; there is no fixed seven-stage checklist. Keep materially different work visible, including substantive brainstorming, synthesis, methods, drafting, or appendix preparation where applicable. For each row specify the output or decision, the author's evidenced choice/revision, and the actual check with its performer and result or limit. Do not collapse the whole process into broad paragraphs or a laundry list of assistance. Combine repeated work only when its treatment and checks remain clear. Preserve only documented order: do not invent that a human checked something before or after applying an edit when the record does not establish that timing.
3. **Controls.** Explain the specific quality controls actually applied and consequential problems corrected, linking each to the task it protected: what was checked against what, by whom, and what changed or remained limited. Suitable evidence may concern claim locators, source status, citation mismatches, author revisions, or measured length; the record determines which belong. Do not substitute generic assurances such as “all references verified” or manufacture a correction to make the account sound rigorous. Avoid repeating the table verbatim. State the actual positive scope of each check; do not append generic caveats about every other possible check when no such claim was made. Keep those coverage notes in the working record unless their omission would materially mislead.
4. **Sample interactions.** Select representative interactions that give meaningful coverage of the recorded stages and substantive AI work, within the actual length limit. Identify the stage and label each instruction as an exact excerpt, a condensed instruction from the session, an author reconstruction, or unavailable. Pair it with a supported response excerpt or an explicitly labelled response summary/reconstruction and, where useful, what the author did with it. Quote a response as an exact excerpt only when its exact words are available; missing raw responses must never produce invented quotations. Condensed instructions may be written as readable instructions but must remain labelled as condensed. Keep safe locators to the underlying records in the working record, not a private transcript dump in the appendix. Explain any material sample-coverage gap in the record and retain it in the appendix when required or necessary for an accurate account.

Treat user-provided examples as references for structure, specificity, and voice, not evidence of this project's tool identity, dates, source counts, author actions, corrections, or completed verification. Do not describe substantive assistance as solely language editing. Distinguish agent checks from confirmed human checks in all four parts. Record preparation of the disclosure itself when applicable; one continuous preparation-and-revision entry is enough. Disclose that assistance proportionately, for example as a brief factual sentence when appropriate; do not automatically create another workflow stage, control, and sample solely to repeat internal finalization status. A pending approval of the current appendix belongs in the working record and draft status, not a mandatory extra process row. Unknown author actions in substantive stages still need a qualified account.

Keep the submission prose focused on the author's actual process. Put internal readiness labels, optional model-history gaps, pending governance decisions, and evidence locators in the working record and handoff rather than repeating them throughout the appendix. Retain material disclosure limits in the appendix when their omission would mislead or the governing requirement calls for them. If a necessary author action or check is unconfirmed, draft the supported structure with a visible provisional marker and a targeted placeholder or qualified account; collect focused confirmation questions in the working record. Do not stop safe useful drafting, imply a complete history, or silently turn pending checks into final attestations. Remove draft markers only after the relevant facts and requirements are resolved.

## Check and hand off the exact candidate

Check every disclosure assertion against the use record and its evidence, and each material recorded use against the disclosure. Check that workflow stages preserve the actual sequence, foreground evidenced human guidance and decisions, distinguish human authorship from wording generation, and keep AI and author actions distinct. Check that substantive drafting remains disclosed, controls name actual checks/results and their performers, and sample labels match their provenance. Confirm that samples meaningfully cover the substantive work rather than selectively showing only minor editing. Verify heading order, absence of duplicate sections, affected-location references, length, required wording and placement, privacy redactions, and consistency with the candidate. Confirm that preparation of the appendix has been added to the record where applicable. Reopen the mapping if the manuscript or disclosure changes.

For substantial review, hand a read-only reviewer the exact candidate path and version/hash, applicable requirements and locators, safe use record/evidence references, unresolved gaps, and bounded checks. Use the [reviewer workflow](../academic-paper-reviewer/SKILL.md) and its [claim audit](../academic-paper-reviewer/CLAIM_AUDIT_TEMPLATE.md) where helpful. Record whether review was by a separate worker or sequential self-review. Respect the [team handoff](../../agents/README.md) and [routing policy](../../agents/routing/MODEL_ROUTING.md); workers do not delegate further.

Report changed paths, candidate identity, evidence coverage, checks actually performed, and remaining policy/history/author-confirmation gaps. A final candidate belongs in `submissions/final/` only after appropriate checks and recorded limitations under its folder guidance. Human confirmation, final approval, and actual submission remain separate states. Do not automatically export, upload, submit, publish, or contact authors or assessors.

## Contextual official guidance

These are University of Melbourne research sources checked on 2026-10-20, with their scope stated below. Recheck the relevant current requirements for the actual task; these links are examples of governing-source discovery, not universal submission rules.

- [Responsible use of AI in research](https://research.unimelb.edu.au/strengths/responsible-research/trusted-research/responsible-use-of-AI-in-research), sections “What responsible use looks like”, “Acknowledgement of digital assistance tools”, “Editing assistance”, and “Content assistance”: distinguish editing from content assistance; document relevant use and affected material; obtain required author confirmations; protect inputs and check outputs. Its contextual minor-editing exception must not erase stricter assessment or venue requirements. Recommended acknowledgement details include tool/version where available, date, and exact prompt; verify plagiarism/copyright concerns and permission for protected inputs.
- [Using AI as a graduate researcher](https://gradresearch.unimelb.edu.au/preparing-my-thesis/graduate-researchers-and-digital-assistance-tools), sections “Text that is produced or altered by GenAI” and “Using GenAI within a thesis”: retain relevant program/date and interaction records, including prompts, uploads, and outputs used. Thesis guidance limits assistance to grammar, clarity and spelling and excludes initial/substantial drafting and English translation. Check its current applicability to the thesis; do not apply those thesis restrictions automatically to other artifacts.
