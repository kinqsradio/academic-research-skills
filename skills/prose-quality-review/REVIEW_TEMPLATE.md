## Main findings

Lead with the most consequential observable issue, or state that no actionable issue was found in the examined scope. Do not infer authorship, calculate a slop score, or manufacture findings. Use short finding blocks when a wide table would be hard to read.

### PQ-01 — Short description

- **Location and exact short excerpt:** section/paragraph/line and quoted text. For omissions, use actual nearby text and identify the places checked.
- **Category and importance:** meaning/grammar, identified requirement, house-style inconsistency, or optional clarity preference; describe the demonstrated consequence.
- **Reader effect:** what becomes unclear, repetitive, misleading, disconnected, or hard to use, for this audience.
- **Support and limits:** manuscript relationship, supplied requirement, or original source/record and precise locator; explicitly mark support unavailable or unverified.
- **Smallest optional correction:** a bounded author action; no rewritten passage unless separately requested and within policy scope.
- **Meaning to preserve:** relevant values/units, scope, uncertainty, negation, attribution/citations, terminology, quotations/code/math, disclosure or proposed/completed distinction.

## Coverage and next decisions

State what was actually read and checked, what remains unverified, and which consequential questions require the full claim/exhibit audit or author input. A prose review is not research validation or final approval. Mention sound conventions worth retaining only when useful to prevent unnecessary edits. Do not reproduce a long generic checklist.

For a requested re-review, record the new candidate and each prior finding's status: addressed / partly addressed / unresolved / no longer applicable, with evidence. Do not reopen settled preferences without a new reason.
