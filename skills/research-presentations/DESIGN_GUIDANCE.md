# Design guidance and evidence boundaries

These sources inform judgment; they do not prescribe one universal slide template. Source verification date: 2026-10-20. Recheck current product instructions when implementing.

The team library previously had no presentation-specific procedure. Host presentation skills supply implementation; this skill supplies academic evidence, storyboarding, and review. The official [Anthropic PPTX skill](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/skills/main/skills/pptx/SKILL.md) was inspected as a tooling comparison, not copied or installed; its host assumptions and style rules are not requirements here. Other available host skills can serve the same implementation role.

| Guidance | Source and precise locator | Scope and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consider messages supported by visual evidence; develop sentence headlines and notes, then practice. | Penn State, [Assertion-Evidence Approach](https://writing.engr.psu.edu/assertion_evidence_EA.html), Steps 1–3. | Original authors' design guidance. Optional for slides communicating supported findings; topic titles remain appropriate elsewhere. |
| Integrated multimedia design can improve understanding in the studied setting. | J. K. Garner and M. P. Alley, [“How the Design of Presentation Slides Affects Audience Comprehension”](https://writing.engr.psu.edu/ae_comprehension.pdf), 2013, abstract p. 1564; Methods, Section 2.1, p. 1569. | Study of 110 undergraduate engineering students viewing MRI instruction compared a bundled assertion–evidence/multimedia intervention with common practice. Reported better comprehension, fewer misconceptions, and delayed recall; it does not isolate headline effects or establish a universal template. |
| Limit projected text, consider viewing distance/contrast/motion, and describe important visuals aloud. | W3C WAI, [Making Events Accessible](https://www.w3.org/WAI/teach-advocate/accessible-presentations/), “Preparing Slides and Projected Material” and “Participants and Speakers: During the Meeting or Presentation.” | Accessibility guidance for presentations/events, including accessible materials beyond PDF alone; not a fixed word or font-count law. |
| Check titles, reading order, alt text, fonts/color, and accessibility findings. | Microsoft Support, [Make your PowerPoint presentations accessible](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accessibility/powerpoint/make-your-powerpoint-presentations-accessible-to-people-with-disabilities), corresponding topic sections and Accessibility Checker. | PowerPoint-specific capabilities/UI vary by version. A checker is partial assistance, not certification. |
| Understand whether editing preserves an Office file or creates a converted copy. | Google Docs Editors Help, [Work with Microsoft Office files](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/9406611?hl=en), edit Office files and convert Office files sections. | Original-format edits autosave in that format; conversion creates a copy. This does not guarantee perfect round-trip fidelity. |

Team workflow choices are: Markdown as canonical slide text/notes; evidence maps; preserving originals; round-trip comparisons; native editability where supported; inspection of every rendered slide; at most two repair passes; and explicit verification limits. These are practical controls, not experimentally proven universal requirements.

Apply hierarchy, whitespace, contrast, and consistent restrained typography to help readers find the message and inspect its evidence. Let content determine layout. Prefer a useful chart, annotated mechanism, or comparison over decorative assets when a visual is needed. Preserve nuance when shortening text; move necessary detail into notes or additional slides within the brief. Estimate timing from content and delivery needs, then distinguish that estimate from human rehearsal. No cited source establishes a universal six-by-six rule, one slide per minute, or mandatory stock imagery.
