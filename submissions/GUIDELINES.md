# Submission guidelines

Use this file on a research branch to record the applicable project, assessment, or venue requirements. No brief or rubric is supplied by the template.

Research drafts and submission text use English by default; change output language only when explicitly requested. Preserve original source titles and quotations for accurate citations and label translations.

## Writing and review folders

- [drafts/](drafts/GUIDELINES.md): outlines, working manuscripts, abstracts, and response-letter drafts.
- [reviews/](reviews/GUIDELINES.md): draft-specific findings, resolution trackers, and re-review records.
- [final/](final/GUIDELINES.md): rendered candidates and companion deliverables with explicit approval status.
- [version/](version/GUIDELINES.md): preserved review and submission snapshots, recorded in [VERSION.md](VERSION.md).

Keep source downloads in papers/ and research planning, evidence, and methods records in the relevant docs/ category. A draft can proceed with visible gaps; final approval requires the applicable requirements and unresolved issues to be checked.

| Due date, time zone, and submission channel | Pending official brief. |
| Collaboration, AI-use permissions, and disclosure wording | Pending official course policy. |
| Ethics or data-handling requirements, if applicable | Pending project scope and official guidance. |

Replace pending entries with verified details and a source link or repository-relative brief path. Record the brief's version or access date. Do not invent requirements or treat a research skill's defaults as course rules.

## Preparing an artifact

- Verify the current brief and policy before finalizing assessed work.
- Build the reference list in IEEE style with numbers assigned by first citation and reused for repeat citations. Use verified metadata from [the paper catalog](../papers/REFERENCES.md); catalog source IDs are not manuscript citation numbers. Check every citation maps to one entry and every listed entry is cited.
- Check that claims are supported by the cited sources and that quotations have accurate locators.
- Identify proposals and anticipated results clearly; do not present them as completed research.
- Check required sections, length, references, formatting, and any permitted-use disclosure.
- Inspect the final rendered document when exporting a format whose layout matters.
- Record validation and unresolved issues in the version record. Preserve previous snapshots in `submissions/version/`.

The human author approves the final artifact and authorizes submission. A completed draft, passing checks, or an AI review does not establish that a submission occurred.
